# Engineering Judgment

> [!abstract] Behavioural, not an HLD round. They still want STAR. Facts and diagrams come from Resume notes; this list is the *questions* an EM will ask about how you work on a backend.

### Tell me about a technical tradeoff you owned. What did you give up?

### Tell me about technical debt you chose to take — or refused to take.

### Tell me about a technology or design you chose and would not choose again.

### Tell me about a time you pushed back on a bad technical decision.

### Tell me about a time you simplified a design. Tell me about a time you made one too complex.

### Tell me about a time you had to work in messy legacy code. How did you avoid making it worse?

### Tell me about a disagreement in code review.

### Tell me about a time you skipped tests or review under pressure. What happened?

### Tell me about a time you added tests, monitoring, or an alert because something hurt.

### Tell me about a performance problem you diagnosed.

### Tell me about a time you estimated badly. What did you change in how you estimate?

### Tell me about rollback vs forward-fix. A time you picked one.

### Tell me about a customer-visible failure and how you communicated it.

### Tell me about a security, privacy, or "this could leak data" moment. What did you do?

### Tell me about a time you were asked to do something you were not comfortable with (shortcut, hide a bug, cook a metric).

### Tell me about a 3 a.m. or weekend call and the decision you made with incomplete information.

### How do you handle being on call? A time you were tired and still had to choose.

### Tell me about a blameless (or blamed) post-mortem you were in. What did you contribute?

### Tell me about a time you improved reliability, not features.

### How do you decide when a "quick hack" is acceptable?

- Also: refactor vs ship the feature this sprint.
